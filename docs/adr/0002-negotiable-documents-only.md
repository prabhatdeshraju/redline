# 0002 — Negotiable documents only; terms of service are out of scope

Redline accepts only documents the reader still has standing to change —
freelance agreements, client contracts, leases, vendor agreements — and refuses
terms of service and other adhesion contracts. We are keeping the drafted
counter-offer, and a counter-offer is meaningless where the only options are
accept or walk away.

## Considered options

The research (`research/summary.md` §5.2) found these two commitments
incompatible: the best-evidenced harms sit in documents nobody can negotiate,
and the counter-offer only works in documents where the evidence is thinnest.
One of the two had to go.

- **Keep terms of service, drop the counter-offer.** Redline becomes "explain
  what you are agreeing to" — a job ToS;DR already does free for pre-curated
  sites, and a general-purpose model does passably for anything else.
- **Keep both, and let the counter-offer be decoration on ToS analysis.**
  Rejected: it teaches readers that the product's most distinctive output is
  something they cannot use.

## Consequences

- We give up the four best-evidenced harms in the entire research set —
  mandatory arbitration (60M+ US workers bound), auto-renewal and
  negative-option traps (FTC complaints 42/day → 70/day, 2021–2024), fee
  escalators and junk fees ($14.5B in card late fees in 2022), and unilateral
  amendment clauses — because none of them is negotiable.
- We are betting on the clause types with the *weakest* frequency evidence
  (freelance nonpayment, personal guarantees, IP assignment, lease termination
  fees), because they are the only ones a counter-offer can act on.
- The reader must arrive **before** signing, which is when they feel the least
  pain and are least aware they need help. Every documented instance of this
  pain in the research is retrospective. Timing and distribution are therefore
  the central product risk, not a marketing detail — and whether anyone pays
  pre-emptively is still untested.
- Uploading a terms-of-service document is a case we have to handle
  deliberately, not an error. Readers will try it.
