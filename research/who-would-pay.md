## Detailed Findings

1. **Average US lawyer hourly rate (general, 2023 data, widely cited into 2025):** "$162 and $392, giving law firms a reliable baseline for setting their fees," median $249/hr, average $257/hr; highest state average Washington D.C. at $392/hr, lowest West Virginia at $162/hr.
   Source: https://www.lawpay.com/about/blog/lawyer-hourly-rate-by-state/

2. **Contract review specific hourly/flat rates:** Attorneys charge $150–$500/hour for contract review specifically; flat fees for standard review typically $300–$3,000 depending on complexity; a typical business or employment contract (5–15 pages) runs $300–$1,500 for a full review; retainer options run $500–$5,000/month for a set number of reviews.
   Source: https://mylegalpal.com/contract-review-cost-what-lawyers-actually-charge/

3. **Residential lease review flat fee:** "The average cost for a lawyer to review a rental lease agreement is $440 on a flat fee" (verbatim), described as an average across all US states based on completed ContractsCounsel platform projects. A second source citing similar data reported $450.
   Source: https://www.contractscounsel.com/b/rental-lease-agreement-cost

4. **Commercial lease review flat fee:** Average $730 flat fee; simple reviews without negotiation run $600–$1,200; complex commercial leases (triple-net clauses, CAM charges, personal guarantees) run $1,000–$3,000; some firms charge $1,500–$2,500 for lease review depending on length/complexity.
   Source: https://www.contractscounsel.com/b/commercial-lease-agreement-cost ; https://www.dasingerlaw.com/flat-fee-commercial-and-residential-lease-review.html

5. **LegalShield subscription pricing (real, published, current):** Personal plans: Basic $39.95/mo (document review up to 15 pages), Advanced $49.95/mo — most popular (up to 25 pages, covers spouse/dependents), Premium $59.95/mo (up to 35 pages, "unlimited research"). Business plans: Small Business Basic $59.95/mo (6 document checks/yr, 25 pages each), Small Business Preferred $99.95/mo — most popular (12 checks/yr, 35 pages each), Small Business Premium $169.95/mo (100 checks/yr, 50 pages each, unlimited lawyer letters/calls). Document review is a headline, tier-differentiating feature across every plan.
   Source: https://www.legalshield.com/pricing

6. **Rocket Lawyer subscription pricing:** Rocket Legal $39.99/month (after 7-day free trial); Rocket Legal+ $239.88/year (~$19.99/mo effective). Other cited tiers: Standard $34.99/mo or $199/yr, Plus $49.99/mo or $249/yr, Pro $64.99/mo or $349/yr.
   Source: https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/legal/learn/rocket-lawyer-review ; https://www.rocketlawyer.com/checkout-plans.rl

7. **LegalZoom Business Advisory / Attorney Plan pricing:** Business Advisory Plan starts at $31.25–$39.09/month depending on billing; includes unlimited 30-minute attorney consultations on new matters plus document reviews; members can hire network attorneys at a 25% discount off normal hourly rates outside the plan.
   Source: https://www.legalzoom.com/attorneys/

8. **Adjacent direct competitor pricing and framing (strongest single data point for Redline's exact positioning):** QwickContractReview.com launched (per an Oct 2025 press release) offering "flat-rate contract reviews for just $99" with 24–48 hour turnaround, plus "monthly subscription plans for frequent users, and bulk packages." Target customers named explicitly: "entrepreneurs, small business owners, and freelancers across the United States," described as "underserved entrepreneurs who may not have access to costly legal services." Founder quote: "Too many small businesses and freelancers sign contracts they don't fully understand — and end up paying the price later," aiming to bridge "the gap between do-it-yourself reading and expensive attorney consultations."
   Source: https://markets.financialcontent.com/clarkebroadcasting.mymotherlode/article/247pressrelease-2025-10-2-qwickcontractreviewcom-delivers-99-contract-reviews-in-48-hours-empowering-small-businesses-and-freelancers-nationwide

9. **Fiverr marketplace pricing for ad hoc contract review:** Fiverr lists lawyers/legal professionals offering contract review services "starting from as low as $20–$100," evidencing a live, functioning low-cost marketplace for this exact task.
   Source: https://block.fiverr.com/hire/contract-review

10. **Freelancer/independent-worker population (segment sizing):** MBO Partners' 2025 State of Independence study: "more than 72 million Americans are now working independently" (72.9M per related coverage), of whom 27.6 million work independently full-time. Upwork's broader survey counts 76.4 million. BLS counted about 9.8 million "unincorporated self-employed" in December 2025 (narrower definition).
   Source: https://www.mbopartners.com/blog/press/2025-state-of-independence-reveals-growing-talent-strategy-for-businesses/ ; https://carry.com/learn/freelancing-statistics

11. **Small business population (segment sizing):** "The U.S. contains 36.2 million small businesses, which account for almost 46 percent of private sector employment" — small businesses represent 99.9% of all US businesses. (2025 Small Business Profiles, SBA Office of Advocacy.)
   Source: https://www.kaplancollectionagency.com/business-advice/54-small-business-statistics-for-2025/ (citing SBA Office of Advocacy 2025 report; primary SBA URL https://advocacy.sba.gov/2025/06/30/new-advocacy-report-shows-the-number-of-small-businesses-in-the-u-s-exceeds-36-million/ returned a connection error on direct fetch but is the report's home page)

12. **Renter household population (segment sizing):** Renter households reached a record 46.1 million in 2025 (one estimate: 46.4 million in Q2 2025, +2.6%); approximately 102.7 million people live in renter-occupied housing, ~31.4% of the US population.
   Source: https://www.self.inc/info/rent-statistics/ ; https://arbor.com/blog/renters-now-represent-80-of-u-s-household-growth/

13. **Access-to-justice affordability gap:** Clio's Legal Trends Report cites the World Justice Project finding that "77% of legal problems don't receive any legal support," and reports that affordability and payment-plan availability are significant factors in whether consumers hire a firm at all (81% said a firm's ability to offer payment plans influences hireability).
   Source: https://www.clio.com/blog/highlights-from-2020-legal-trends-report/
