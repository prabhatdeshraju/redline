# What Goes Wrong: Clause Types That Burn People (Evidence-Ranked)

Research for Redline. Compiled from regulator actions/data, litigation, and survey data on contract/lease/freelance-agreement/ToS clauses. Search budget used: 12 web searches, 3 pages fetched (within the 12-search / 15-page caps). Stopped once 8+ distinct sourced findings were reached.

## Detailed Findings

1. **Negative-option/auto-renewal complaints are rising sharply and are now a top FTC enforcement priority.** The FTC received an average of ~42 complaints/day about negative-option and subscription practices in 2021, rising to ~70/day in 2024 — over 100,000 complaints in five years. Common themes: unclear enrollment disclosures and unauthorized charges. Source: [FTC, "Federal Trade Commission Announces Final Click-to-Cancel Rule," Oct 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/10/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-click-cancel-rule-making-it-easier-consumers-end-recurring).

2. **Amazon paid $2.5 billion to settle FTC claims it used "dark patterns" to enroll and trap Prime subscribers**, including deliberately convoluted cancellation flows — one of the largest FTC settlements in the agency's history (settled Sept 2025, case originally filed June 2023). Source: [Zwillgen](https://www.zwillgen.com/ftc-state-ag/multi-billion-dollar-amazon-auto-renewal-settlement-importance-subscription-compliance/), [Regulatory Oversight](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2025/10/amazon-settles-ftc-lawsuit-highlighting-regulatory-scrutiny-of-subscription-practices/).

3. **Adobe paid $150 million (plus consumer refunds) to resolve FTC/DOJ claims it hid early-termination fees and made cancellation deliberately difficult** for subscribers to Creative Cloud plans (complaint filed June 2024). Source: [Law Commentary](https://www.lawcommentary.com/articles/adobe-to-pay-150-million-to-resolve-federal-claims-over-hidden-subscription-termination-fees).

4. **Mandatory arbitration and class-action waivers are near-universal in consumer financial contracts.** CFPB's arbitration study found over 90% of studied consumer-finance arbitration clauses also contained class-action waivers. A 2008 study of internet/phone/data contracts found 75% had arbitration clauses and 80% had class-action waivers. In employment, 56.2% of nonunion private-sector employees (60M+ workers) are covered by forced arbitration. Source: [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-study-finds-that-arbitration-agreements-limit-relief-for-consumers/), [EPI](https://www.epi.org/publication/the-growing-use-of-mandatory-arbitration-access-to-the-courts-is-now-barred-for-more-than-60-million-american-workers/).

5. **The National Association of Consumer Advocates (NACA) published a March 2024 report, "Fine Print Traps," that independently ranks the boilerplate clauses causing the most consumer harm**, overlapping with what the CFPB preliminarily flagged in its 2023 nonbank-contract-terms registry proposal. Its list, in the order presented: (A) forced/pre-dispute arbitration, (B) class-action waivers, (C) pre-dispute jury-trial waivers, (D) shortened statutes of limitations, (E) waiver of substantive rights/remedies ("remedy-stripping" clauses), (F) choice-of-law/forum-selection clauses, (G) limitation of liability/damages caps, (H) non-required pre-dispute resolution processes (used partly to block mass arbitration), (I) loser-pays provisions, (J) unilateral amendment clauses, (K) confidentiality/secrecy provisions. This is the single most directly on-point ranked source found for Redline's purpose. Source: [NACA, "Fine Print Traps," Christine Hines, March 2024](https://www.consumeradvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NACA_fineprinttraps_mostharm032024.pdf).

6. **Unilateral amendment clauses ("we may change these terms at any time") generate frequent consumer complaints but comparatively little litigation**, per legal-industry commentary — likely because the harm shows up gradually and individually, not in one discrete triggering event. Documented bad-faith uses include Ticketmaster attempting to retroactively rewrite its arbitration process mid-dispute (*Heckman v. Live Nation*) and Heartland Payment Systems adding forced-arbitration/class-waiver terms mid-dispute with parents over school-lunch program fees (*Story v. Heartland*). Source: [NACA report §J](https://www.consumeradvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NACA_fineprinttraps_mostharm032024.pdf), [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e79c3c06-ec1e-4040-934f-b0fb21d54eb0).

7. **Non-compete clauses cover roughly 30 million U.S. workers (18% of the workforce)**, per FTC estimates underlying its April 2024 nationwide ban. The ban was blocked by a federal district court in August 2024 and the FTC dropped its appeal in September 2025, so non-competes remain broadly enforceable today even though the prevalence and worker-mobility-harm data are well-documented. Source: [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-rule-banning-noncompetes).

8. **Freelance nonpayment and scope creep are pervasive per Freelancers Union survey data (NY freelancers):** 62% report lost wages from nonpayment at least once in their career, 91% report late/overdue payment at least once (54% with delays of 3+ months), 53% report losing up to $10,000 total to nonpayment, and an earlier study found 71% have struggled to collect payment for work at least once. A more recent Freelancers Union figure states 67% of freelancers report doing unpaid work because of scope creep. Source: [Freelancers Union](https://blog.freelancersunion.org/2022/05/12/over-60-of-ny-freelancers-report-not-being-paid-for-work-performed/), [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/survey-finds-62-percent-of-ny-freelance-workers-have-lost-wages-due-to-nonpayment/).

9. **Credit-card late fees ("junk fees") cost consumers roughly $14.5 billion in 2022 alone (a 28% YoY jump)**, prompting a March 2024 CFPB rule to cut the safe-harbor late fee from $32 to $8 for large issuers; roughly 1 in 5 Americans (~52 million people) paid a card late fee in the prior year per a 2023 Consumer Reports survey cited by CFPB-adjacent sources. Source: [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-bans-excessive-credit-card-late-fees-lowers-typical-fee-from-32-to-8/), [Center for American Progress](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/the-cfpb-is-cleaning-up-junk-fees/).

## What I Could Not Find

- **No aggregated, cross-category frequency ranking** (e.g., "X% of disputes involve clause type Y") that directly compares clause types against each other across contract categories (leases, freelance agreements, ToS, B2B) in one dataset. The NACA report is the closest thing to a ranked list, but it ranks by *harm severity as argued by the author*, not by measured complaint frequency across categories.
- **No CFPB Consumer Complaint Database query results with clause-level tagging** — the CFPB's public complaint database categorizes by product/issue (e.g., "billing dispute," "problem with fees"), not by specific contract clause type, so a true frequency count per clause type was not obtainable within the search budget.
- **No hard statistics on indemnification clauses** specifically (as distinct from limitation-of-liability) — found only general contract-drafting guidance, no complaint or litigation-frequency data.
- **No hard statistics on notice-period traps** or "kill fee" disputes specifically in freelance contracts, beyond the general nonpayment/scope-creep survey data above.
- **No frequency data on lease-specific automatic-renewal clauses** for residential tenants (as distinct from the well-documented consumer-subscription auto-renewal data) — only general state AG guidance on lease terms was found.
- **No verifiable, specifically-sourced statistic on the prevalence of "work for hire" language in freelance/tech-services contracts** — a plausible-sounding stat surfaced in search results but could not be traced to one specific, citable URL, so it was dropped per the sourcing guardrail rather than included on weak attribution.
- **Current enforcement status of the FTC's Negative Option Rule and CFPB late-fee rule is in flux** (court challenges, agency reversals as of 2025-2026) — the underlying complaint-volume and cost data cited above predate/are independent of that litigation status, but readers should not assume either rule is currently in force.
