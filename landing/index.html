Redline reads a negotiable document — a client agreement, statement of work, vendor or service agreement, or lease, anything you still have standing to change — and returns a numbered schedule of flags, ranked by the worst outcome each clause permits. Every flag quotes the sentence it came from, so you can find it in your own copy and check it yourself.

Inspect your own document

Example inspection — the agreement quoted below was written for this page, not a real client’s document.

  1. What it permits at worst

    Your own assets — not your company’s — can be used to satisfy a claim under this agreement. The guarantee outlives the agreement that created it.

    Whether a court would enforce it, and against which assets, depends on your jurisdiction and how the guarantee was executed.

    Counter-offer to send back

    Delete clause 12.4. If the Client needs security, offer instead: “The Contractor’s liability under this Agreement is limited to the fees paid or payable under it, and no personal guarantee is given.”

  2. What it permits at worst

    You pay the Client’s legal costs and losses for a third party’s claim even where you did nothing wrong, with no ceiling and no relation to what you were paid.

    Courts do read some indemnities more narrowly than they are written; how narrowly depends on the wording and the jurisdiction.

    Counter-offer to send back

    Tie it to fault and cap it: “The Contractor shall indemnify the Client against claims arising from the Contractor’s gross negligence or wilful misconduct, up to the total fees paid under this Agreement.”

  3. What it permits at worst

    The tooling you arrived with, and everything you build alongside the deliverable, transfers to the Client on creation. Reuse and portfolio rights go with it.

    Most commissioned freelance work does not legally qualify as work made for hire even where a contract says it is; what the clause then achieves varies.

    Counter-offer to send back

    Separate the deliverable from your toolkit: “The Client owns the deliverables on payment in full. The Contractor retains ownership of pre-existing and general-purpose tools, libraries and know-how, and grants the Client a licence to use them as embedded in the deliverables.”

What was looked for

The list is fixed and published. You can see what was inspected and what was not — which is the only thing that makes a clean result mean anything.

Key to the marks

Highest severity
Unbounded, or reaching your own assets.
High severity
Bounded, but it can cost you your ownership, your remedy, or your ability to earn.
Medium severity
Bounded by the value of the agreement.

Rank is what a clause permits at worst, never how common it is. Denser hatch, worse worst case.

  2. 2 Uncapped indemnification Flagged · highest

  8. 8 Scope and change control Inspected · not found

A clause that is not on this list is not looked for. A risk that comes from something the document does not say — no notice period, no payment terms, no liability cap — has no sentence to quote, so it is not flagged either.

You can also declare terms you will not accept, in your own words. Redline treats a match as disqualifying however that clause would otherwise rank.

All clear

When nothing matches, that is the result you get.

An all-clear arrives with the full list of what was checked and found absent. Never an empty screen, and never padded with a manufactured top three so the inspection looks like it earned its keep.

Limitations of this inspection

  • No verdict

    Redline never tells you whether to sign, and it is not legal advice. It is flat about what the document says and hedged about what that means.

  • Negotiable documents only

    Terms of service and other take-it-or-leave-it documents are out of scope. A counter-offer needs someone on the other side who can accept it.

  • Typed text only

    Scanned or photographed documents are not inspected. A misread character produces a quotation that is wrong in the one way you could not catch.

  • Before you sign

    Replacement wording is worth nothing once the document is signed. Redline is for the moment the terms can still change.

  • Worst case, not likelihood

    Late payment is the most common harm a freelancer meets. It still ranks below a personal guarantee, because ranking is by what a clause permits at worst.

  • No sentence, no flag

    Where the exact wording cannot be quoted, the flag is dropped rather than approximated. That is a defect to fix, not a result to serve you.
