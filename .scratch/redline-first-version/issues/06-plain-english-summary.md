# 06: Plain-English summary

**What to build:** Before looking at individual risks, a reader sees what the
document commits them to, in language they can read without a lawyer. Their
obligations and the other side's are stated separately, so the asymmetry of the
deal is visible at a glance. The money, the dates and the duration are named,
so the reader can check the commercial terms against what was agreed verbally.

The summary describes only what the document says. Where the text does not
support a claim, the summary does not make it.

**Blocked by:** 02.

**Status:** ready-for-agent

- [ ] A reader sees a plain-English summary of what the document commits them to
- [ ] The reader's obligations and the other side's are stated separately
- [ ] Payment amounts, dates and duration are named where the document states them
- [ ] The summary avoids legal jargon a non-lawyer would have to look up
- [ ] The summary asserts nothing the document does not say
- [ ] Statements about what the document says are flat and unhedged; statements about what a clause means legally carry their uncertainty
